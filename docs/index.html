Charts that live on your machine.

momo charts is a stock charting and screening app for swing traders that runs on your own computer. Watchlists, drawings, indicators, a screener for every US stock and price alerts, with live prices and 20 years of daily history from your own Schwab account.

Loaded before you ask

Step through a watchlist or screener with ↓ or Space and momo has already loaded the next two charts and the one before (in split view, for both timeframes). Rest the mouse on a row for a moment and its chart and live quote start loading, so the click shows both at once.

~12 ms

Read from your own disk

Daily history sits in a SQLite database on your machine, not behind someone else's API. Five years of daily bars for a stock come back in about 12 milliseconds on a desktop, even when nothing is loaded ahead.

Once

Loaded once, shared everywhere

Each chart's bars are loaded once and shared by the chart, split view and the details panel. Live quotes update them in place, so nothing reloads while the market moves.

How the data works

Nothing passes through us.

There is no momo server. The app on your computer talks to Schwab for prices and to free public sources for everything else, and keeps it all in a folder you own.

bars.db: about 20 years a symbol, split-adjusted. The first full fill takes about an hour; any chart you open fills at once. After each close momo adds the day's bar and catches splits. Expect 1 to 3 GB.

momo.db: your watchlists, drawings, chart settings, saved screens and alerts. Back it up like any file.

live.toml: your Schwab app keys and sign-in, readable only by your user account. They never leave your machine.

Install

Running in about two minutes.

All you install is uv; it fetches the right Python on its own. The Schwab app takes a few days to be approved, so start that first.

  1. $curl -LsSf https://astral.sh/uv/install.sh | shInstalls uv. Skip if you have it.
  2. $git clone https://github.com/alx-kap/momo-charts.git && cd momo-charts
  3. $uv run momo serveInstalls dependencies on first run, then starts the server.

Then open http://127.0.0.1:8790. The Market data window asks for your Schwab keys.

Connect Schwab, once

  1. At developer.schwab.com, sign in with your Schwab login and create an app with Market Data Production (add Accounts and Trading Production to stream quotes). Callback https://127.0.0.1:8182, order limit 0.
  2. Wait for approval, usually a few days. The app page then shows an App Key and a Secret.
  3. Paste both into momo's Market data window and click Save and connect.
  4. Click Sign in to Schwab…, approve, and paste back the address of the page that fails to load. It lasts 7 days; the live badge warns a day ahead.
Questions

Before you clone.

Does it place trades?

No. momo only reads market data. The Schwab app it uses is created with an order limit of 0.

Do I need a Schwab account?

Today, yes: Schwab supplies the prices and history, and the Trader API's market data comes with a brokerage account at no extra cost. Other providers can be added: implement daily() history and sweep() quotes like live/schwab.py, or fill bars.db yourself.

Where does it run?

Anywhere Python 3.12 runs. It's developed on Linux and used from a browser on a Mac. The server listens on your own computer only (127.0.0.1) unless you choose otherwise.

How do I get earnings dates and fundamentals?

Run uv run momo fundamentals-update (SEC EDGAR and Nasdaq). SEC asks for a contact address: set user_agent = "momo you@example.com" under [fundamentals] in config/momo.toml. Futures positioning is uv run momo cot-update.

Is it affiliated with TradingView or Schwab?

No. Charts are drawn with TradingView Lightweight Charts™, used under its Apache 2.0 licence. Schwab data is used through your own developer app.
